Measure the room and confirm the hardware before buying stock or cutting parts. The current design is a horizontal queen bed with one cabinet on the room-facing left—not two bookcases.

2. Survey the room with the installer

  1. Measure the wall at floor, middle and cabinet-top height. Record floor level, wall plumb, ceiling height, adjacent trim, outlets, switches, vents, windows and door swings. Allow installation access; the 116.75 in carcass width is not a recommended tight wall opening.
  2. Locate the actual structure. A professional or qualified installer must verify at least three 2 × 4 or larger wooden wall studs for the queen bed and determine actual anchor positions. The illustrated stud positions are not a drilling template. Drywall anchors, metal-stud substitution and brick/masonry installation are prohibited by this kit’s instructions.
  3. Resolve baseboard and flush-to-wall contact. Rockler calls for cutting the base molding so the cabinet can sit flush. The installer must reconcile leveling/plumbing shims with the requirement that each top wall bracket sits flush to both the top and wall, with no gap. Do not add a stand-off detail or notch a structural panel from this drawing alone.
  4. Confirm the full deployed envelope with the actual kit. Include frame, door panels, legs, handles, bedding, the open drawer and people walking around the bed. Check collisions throughout travel and preserve safe circulation and exit access. The model’s open view is a review aid—not a certified reach dimension.
  5. Plan two independent restraints. The bed needs both headboards anchored plus all three top wall brackets. The left cabinet needs its own suitable anti-tip restraint into structure; its ¼ in back and optional cabinet connector bolts are not substitutes.

Installation authority: Rockler p. 3, safety and wall requirements; pp. 11–12, headboard anchors, base molding and top brackets. If the room cannot meet those requirements, stop and consult Rockler and the installer; this site does not approve an alternative anchoring system.

3. Plan stock handling and the build location

  1. Check the route for full 48 × 96 in sheets. Measure vehicle/loading access, door openings, stair turns and the route to the machine. Arrange suitable help and handling equipment; do not assume a finished 86.75 in-wide bed carcass will fit through the same route.
  2. Choose where assembly happens. Confirm that the glued bed carcass can reach the room, or plan an appropriate in-room assembly area before glue-up. Reserve supported, level work space for large door panels and safe access around the cabinet.
  3. Store panels flat and supported in a dry area. Follow the supplier’s conditioning guidance, identify veneer faces and grain direction, and record thickness by stock lot before generating final machining geometry.
  4. Keep sheet IDs and offcuts organized. The current allowance is six ¾ in full sheets, one ½ in 24 × 48 in project panel, and one ¼ in full sheet. Shared sheets serve both assemblies; no spare full sheet is included. See the purchase guide before ordering.
